Why Ownvesting

Most investing tools give you more information. We want to help you build more understanding.

The hard part is not finding one more ratio, article or target price. It is knowing which facts matter, what they imply, which assumptions you are making, and what would prove your thesis wrong.

From fact to conviction

Keep the reasoning chain intact.

Evidence is useful when you can see how it became an investment conclusion — and where judgment, assumptions or uncertainty entered the chain.

EvidenceWhat was actually reported, disclosed or observed?
InterpretationWhat does that evidence imply about the business?
AssumptionWhat are we expecting that is not yet a fact?
ConvictionHow much should this matter to the investment case?

The investment dimensions

Not every company needs the same valuation method, but every investment needs a disciplined set of questions.

BusinessRevenue model, segment economics, customer concentration, cyclicality and structural drivers.
ManagementCapital allocation, governance, execution quality, incentives and what management does when conditions change.
Financial qualityMargins, balance-sheet strength, cash conversion, reinvestment needs and return on incremental capital.
ExpectationsWhat the current price appears to require from growth, margins, duration and risk.
ValuationDCF, multiples, SOTP, NAV or another method chosen because it fits the business — not because it is convenient.

OwnChek

Ask better questions before conviction becomes ownership.

OwnChek is the conversational layer of Ownvesting. Ask what you are worried about, what you do not understand, or what you think the market is missing. It connects the question to the relevant investment dimension and points toward the next evidence worth checking.

“Revenue is still growing. Why should I care that operating cash flow is weak?”
Because growth can look healthy while cash conversion deteriorates. The next check is whether receivables, inventory or one-off working-capital movements explain the gap — and whether the pattern is temporary or persistent.
“Does that change the thesis?”
Not automatically. It changes the burden of proof. If cash conversion keeps weakening while reported profit rises, earnings quality becomes a material thesis risk worth monitoring.
OwnChek guides research. It does not infer that you own a stock just because you ask about it.
